# Keep the Words You Almost Used

Most of the friction in writing comes from choosing too early. You pick a word, the sentence moves on, and the other candidates quietly disappear. A week later you can’t remember what they were, only that one of them was better.

Editors have always known this. A good editor doesn’t erase your sentence. They write a suggestion in the margin and let you decide. The page holds both versions until you’re ready, and you judge them the only way that works: in context, with the real sentence around them.

Software forgot this. Most editors treat writing as a single line that only moves forward. Undo takes you back, but only by throwing away everything after. Version control keeps history, but history is the wrong shape: it remembers when you changed something, not what you were choosing between.

So this tool keeps the alternatives. Any word, sentence or paragraph can hold several versions. Hover over one, press the arrow keys, and read each version in place. A soft tone tells you you’ve wandered off the original. A lower one tells you you’re back.

The same goes for cutting. When you’re unsure about a sentence, cut it instead of deleting it. I’ve lost too many good lines to the delete key. A cut stays on the page, faded, easy to skip and easy to bring back. Anything that doesn’t belong yet waits beside the page, one keystroke away.

Nothing is lost. You just decide later.
